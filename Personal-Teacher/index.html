Skills I Have

Technical Simplification & Pedagogical Translation

The ability to take abstract, complex computing concepts such as network protocols, recursion, database normalization, or binary logic and break them down using relatable, real-world analogies and structured scaffolding. IT subjects can easily overwhelm beginners with heavy jargon. By translating technical mechanics into everyday mental models (e.g., comparing an API to a restaurant waiter or RAM to a physical desk), you bridge the gap between confusion and comprehension, ensuring students grasp how and why systems work rather than just memorizing syntax.

Hands-On Troubleshooting & Live Debugging

Diagnosing hardware, software, network, and code issues in real time—often under classroom time constraints—while walking students through the logical process of elimination. In a lab environment, things inevitably break: code throws unexpected exceptions, virtual machines fail to boot, or network configurations conflict. Instead of simply fixing the issue for the student, you model systematic problem-solving (reading error logs, isolating variables, testing hypotheses), teaching them resilience and independent debugging skills they will use throughout their tech careers.

Curriculum Adaptation & Continuous Upskilling

Constantly updating lesson plans, lab exercises, and teaching tools to keep pace with the rapid evolution of the technology landscape, while tailoring instruction to varying skill levels within the same classroom. Unlike static subjects, IT changes every year—new programming frameworks emerge, cybersecurity threats evolve, and tools like AI reshape workflows. Your ability to continuously learn new technologies, integrate relevant industry practices into your syllabus, and differentiate instruction (challenging advanced coders while supporting beginners) keeps your classroom relevant and prepares students for the modern workforce.

Universitas Brawijaya Universitas Indonesia Institut Teknologi Bandung

Educational of Information Technology

The Information Technology Education Study Program (Program Studi Pendidikan Teknologi Informasi or PTI) is housed within the Department of Information Systems at the Faculty of Computer Science, Universitas Brawijaya (FILKOM UB) in Malang. Designed to bridge core computing expertise with pedagogical mastery, the program prepares students to become both competent IT professionals and innovative educators in the digital era. Its curriculum integrates foundational computer science and information technology subjects—such as programming, databases, computer networks, and multimedia development—with instructional design, curriculum development, educational psychology, and e-learning technologies.

Beyond classroom theory, PTI students gain practical experience through micro-teaching labs, educational software development projects, and teaching internships (Pengenalan Lapangan Persekolahan) at vocational high schools (SMK) and secondary institutions. Supported by FILKOM UB’s modern computing facilities and digital media laboratories, graduates are uniquely versatile—equipped to pursue careers as ICT educators and instructional designers, as well as educational technology (EdTech) developers, IT trainers, and multimedia specialists across both academic and corporate sectors.

Computer Science

The Computer Science Study Program (Program Studi Ilmu Komputer) at the Faculty of Computer Science, Universitas Indonesia (Fasilkom UI), is the oldest and most established computing program in the country, tracing its roots back to the founding of Pusilkom UI in 1972. Operating primarily at the Depok campus, the program offers comprehensive academic pathways across Bachelor's (S1), Master's (S2), and Doctoral (S3) levels, alongside a prestigious International Class (Kelas Internasional) that enables students to earn dual degrees with partner universities such as The University of Queensland, the University of Birmingham, and the Australian National University. Its curriculum emphasizes deep analytical rigor in algorithms, computational theory, software engineering, artificial intelligence, and system architecture.

Research and academic life in UI’s Computer Science program are driven by specialized laboratories covering Machine Learning and Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing, Reliable Software Engineering, Computer Systems, and Cybersecurity. Students benefit from close integration with industry-backed research centers—including the Tokopedia-UI AI Center of Excellence—as well as an active competitive programming and scientific publishing culture. Renowned for producing top-tier analytical thinkers, the program consistently graduates leading researchers, senior software engineers, and prominent technology startup founders in Indonesia and abroad.

Informatic Engineering

At Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB), core computer science education is delivered through the Informatics Engineering Study Program (Program Studi Teknik Informatika) under the School of Electrical Engineering and Informatics (STEI ITB). Established as a standalone department in 1982 before merging into STEI in 2006, it is widely recognized as one of Indonesia's most selective and prestigious engineering programs. The curriculum blends strong mathematical and algorithmic foundations with intensive practical training in software engineering, distributed systems, computer security, and artificial intelligence, complemented by an International Undergraduate Program (IUP) offering specialized tracks in Artificial Intelligence and Cybersecurity.

Supported by STEI ITB’s specialized Research Groups (Kelompok Keahlian)—notably in Software Engineering, Knowledge Engineering, and Control and Computer Systems—the program fosters a culture of high-level technical innovation and multidisciplinary problem-solving. Students frequently collaborate with peers across electrical, telecommunications, and biomedical engineering while excelling in national and international programming competitions. Graduates of ITB’s Informatics Engineering program are heavily sought after across Southeast Asia and globally as software architects, AI researchers, system engineers, and technical leaders.
